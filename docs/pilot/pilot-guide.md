# Add a Paw | First portrait pilot

Use this guide with `pilot-tracker.xlsx`. Invite ten cat or dog owners in the US or Canada who can test in English. Aim for five permissioned website stories, including both species. Recruit people with different coat colors and markings, including someone who typically takes indoor phone photos. The first five people who have never seen the site can also do the first-impression check.

## Invitation

> Would you try a new website for pet owners on your phone and tell us what feels clear or confusing? It takes about 10–15 minutes. Please have a photo you took of your cat or dog handy. You can keep your feedback private. If you’re interested, reply with a time that suits you.

For the five first-impression checks, send the page link at the start of the test. Keep the portrait, price and upload prompts until after the five-second questions.

## Five-second first impression

Show the English homepage on the participant’s own device for five seconds, then hide it. Ask each question verbatim without offering choices.

1. “What would you start with?”
2. “What would you get at the end?”
3. “What, if anything, would you expect to pay for that?”

Record the words before assigning scores. Count answers as correct when the participant identifies their pet’s photo, a digital portrait they can keep, and a free digital result. Show the page again and say, “Please show me where you’d start and take it as far as the place you add your photo.” Score navigation when they reach the correct uploader without help. On the **First look** tab, score each item independently. The proposed threshold is four of five participants passing all four items. Record any assumption that the result is a physical print or that card details are necessary.

## Free portrait session

Ask if you may take private notes. If you plan to record, ask separately and record only after the person agrees. Keep screens with contact or login details out of recordings.

> “Imagine you found this site on your own. Use a photo you took of your pet, choose a style you like, and try to make something you’d keep. Tell me what you’re looking for as you go.”

1. **Start at the homepage.** For a first-look participant, continue from the uploader they reached. Capture the device, chosen style and where they hesitated. If they say they expect a charge, write down which screen or wording gave that impression.
2. **Follow the actual upload.** Mark upload successful only when the site accepts the photo. Mark preview successful after they can see their generated portrait. Record login prompts, limits, errors and retries where they happen. If they request help, mark subsequent assisted milestones as assisted and write what guidance was given.
3. **Review the result.** Ask “What looks most like your pet?” and “Is there any detail that you’d change?” If they want to change it, let them try and note the result. Ask “Would you save it, share it, or try another style? What makes you say that?” Record their words, including reservations.
4. **Check the download.** If they choose to save it, ask them to find and open the downloaded file. If they prefer to stop, record the reason. Keep assisted, independent and unattempted outcomes separate.
5. **Invite optional publication.** Ask only after the experience. Say “Could we show you a draft of a story about your portrait? You can choose which parts we may share and where.” A yes here allows you to show them a draft. Obtain the item-specific choices below for publication.

## Publish a case

Use **Cases** to link the participant, exact style, original photo, actual output, feedback and proof. Before release, check that the output was generated from that original, the quote matches the participant’s words and both faces remain recognizable in the mobile crop. For a text-and-image case, require explicit permission for the original, portrait and quote for the intended channel. A named credit also needs its own yes. Use an anonymous credit when preferred.

Label the published card **Free digital portrait experience**. A useful card pairs the two images, the owner’s observation and a link to the same style. Change a quote or add a new crop only after the participant has seen and approved the revision. Keep drafts, access details and names used in private correspondence out of public assets. Follow the brand’s current guidance for likeness, photo use and example scenes. [Photo guide](https://luna.nodesk.tech/photo-guide) · [Privacy information](https://luna.nodesk.tech/privacy)

## Read the results

Keep pilot results separate from production analytics. For the website’s first-preview rate, compare unique homepage visitors who later see their first successful preview against unique homepage visitors in the same entry cohort and observation window. Count a visitor once and use the same measurement consent, identity rules and window before and after rollout. Report the observable audience and sample size. In test runs, review the drop between the first CTA, accepted upload, successfully displayed preview and confirmed download. Segment by device and entry style and annotate outages, login prompts and quota errors.
